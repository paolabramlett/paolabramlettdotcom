How I work

A clear process, so you’re never guessing.

Every engagement follows the same four steps, scaled to your budget and timeline. You always know what’s next and what you’ll get.

  1. 01

    Discover

    We align on the problem, the users and the business goal. I ask the important questions — including the uncomfortable ones — before any pixels.

    You get: Problem statement, priorities, scope

  2. 02

    Define

    I map the flows and structure of the product and decide, with you, what belongs in the first version and what can wait.

    You get: User flows, information architecture, MVP scope

  3. 03

    Design

    High-fidelity UI designed as a system — components, states and edge cases — and validated with a clickable prototype.

    You get: Figma designs, design system, prototype

  4. 04

    Deliver

    A clean handoff your developers can build from, or a working front-end if we build it together. I can stay involved through implementation.

    You get: Dev-ready specs or front-end code

FAQ

Questions founders ask me.

How do you price projects?

Most projects are quoted at a fixed price after a free intro call, so you know the scope and investment before we start. Pricing depends on complexity and on what you need delivered — design only, or design plus front-end. I'll always recommend the leanest version that gets you to your goal.

How long does a typical project take?

A landing page or simple prototype takes 3–7 days. A full UX/UI design for an app typically runs 2–6 weeks. Timeline depends on scope and whether development is included.

What does a Senior Product Designer do that a UI designer doesn't?

A UI designer makes screens look right. As a senior product designer, I also help decide which screens should exist: clarifying the problem, prioritizing the MVP, mapping flows and edge cases, and designing a system your team can build and grow. Great visuals are part of the job, not the whole job.

Can you design AI-powered products?

Yes. I've designed products where AI turns user data into specific recommendations — for parents trying to connect with their kids, and for fleet managers trying to prevent truck failures. The hard part is rarely the model: it's collecting good input without friction, and presenting output people trust and act on.

Do you work with clients outside Mexico?

Yes. Most clients are in the US, Canada, and Europe. All work is done remotely, in English or Spanish, across time zones.

Do you handle development or just design?

Both. I design in Figma and build with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and AI-assisted development tools like Claude Code (what some people call vibe coding). You can get design files, a working prototype, or production-ready front-end — or all three.

What kinds of projects are the best fit?

Founders building a first product or MVP, domain experts — like therapists or operations leaders — turning their knowledge into software, and teams whose product has become hard to use. I do my best work when the problem is complex and clarity matters.

What tools do you use?

Figma for product design, prototyping and design systems; Claude Code for AI-assisted development; Adobe Illustrator for brand and visual work; and HTML, CSS and JavaScript for front-end builds.

When is the right time to bring you in?

As early as possible. Design decisions made before development starts prevent expensive rework later. I can join at the idea stage, mid-project, or as an ongoing design partner for an existing team.
